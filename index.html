Restricted Access Notice

The IRMP Knowledge Hub and Dashboards contain protected and confidential information, including Indigenous Knowledge. Access is restricted to IRMP Task Team members only. Content must not be shared, distributed, or reproduced beyond the Task Team without prior authorization.

If partners require briefing materials for their organizations, please contact victoria.fleming-chartier@pc.gc.ca .

About this Knowledge Hub

This knowledge hub presents the outcomes of the Wood Buffalo National Park World Heritage Site Action Plan. It provides access to dashboards, maps, datasets, and story maps on various ecological assessment indicators, offering insights into the park's ecosystem and supporting conservation efforts.

Co-development of the Program
2019 – MSIKTT & The Integrated Research & Monitoring Program (IRMP) Begins

Monitoring, Science, & Indigenous Knowledge Task Team (MSIKTT) established to support the Monitoring & Science theme of the Action Plan.

WBS team drafts inventory of existing and potential indicators pulled together from existing programs & pre-existing monitoring in the PAD:

  • AECOM PAD Science & Monitoring Review
  • WBNP SEA (Strategic Environmental Assessment)
  • WBNP EIM Program
  • CBM Programs (ACFN, MCFN)
  • ECCC/OSM

Community values, concerns, and Way of Life taken into consideration when drafting list of indicators.

January 2023 – IRMP Community Workshop #2
  • Community affirmed River Flow as an indicator
  • All 4 rivers were chosen by community
  • CBM’s monitoring framework was adopted & affirmed by community (Access, Habitat/Abundance, Quality)
November 2023 – IRMP Community Workshop #3

Community influenced structure & analytical framework for River Flow Index to address community concerns such as:

  • Low water for navigation during open water season
  • Low water in PAD, negatively impacting ecology (plants and wildlife)
  • Sudden drops of water in winter = unsafe ice travel
  • Sudden increases in winter = overflow & unsafe ice travel
  • Freshet flows limiting flooding and access to territory
March 2024 – Muskrat Camp
  • Scientists join Elders & Knowledge Holders out on the land to listen & learn from the local knowledge
  • Discussions with community members influence science decisions & guide revisions for River Flow indicator
April 2024 – Nipîy Tu Knowledge Forum
  • Elders’ Circle: experiences, observations, & conclusions shared with scientists
  • Community IK informs reference period selection:
    • Community said things were good in the 50’s–70’s, lots of water (Athabasca & Birch rivers Reference Period)
    • Community said Peace was heavily altered post-dam vs. pre-dam (Peace River & Slave River Reference Period)
2024–2025 – Indicator Refinement
  • Deconstructed & rebuilt the River Flow Index based on input from Daniel Peters, community & IK
  • New refined River Flow indicator still needs to be presented to community for review & feedback
2025 – Co-developed Assessment Results

The IHA’s assessed capture many issues community shared with IRMP team:

  • Poor ice quality on Peace River from sediment in ice (i.e., winter flows too high)
  • Sudden water level fluctuations (both up & down)
  • Lower peak flows producing fewer floods
  • Lower summer minimum flows impacting navigation

IRMP River Flow Index assessed the Peace River as being in “Poor” condition, largely because ice-cover season minimum flows & open water season max flow are no longer reflective of historical conditions.
